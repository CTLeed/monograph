About the Publication

On writing one essay
at a time.

Monograph is a weekly editorial on the software worth studying. It publishes one essay every Tuesday — no more, no less, no thematic crossovers or special series. A newsletter for people who enjoy the long look.

Since Spring 2024. Written in Brooklyn. Printed, once a year, on Mohawk Superfine.

Monograph is an attempt to take software seriously. It treats each product it covers as a work made by people, under constraints, with a specific point of view — and it tries to understand those decisions the way a critic understands a novel or a building. The essays are not reviews; they are readings.

The publication is small on purpose. One essay a week, one piece of software, one author. The constraint is the point: a weekly cadence is just enough time to think carefully and not so much that the work drifts. Monograph is, in its way, an argument against the rolling slurry of software commentary — against the post-hoc frameworks and the charts that explain yesterday. We are trying to do less, and mean more by it.

A newsletter for people who still believe that one well-chosen sentence beats five well-intentioned bullet points.

The Process

Each Monday the editor begins research: reading documentation, interviewing the people involved where possible, using the product under examination for at least a week of real work. The essay is drafted Monday night, edited Tuesday morning, proofed by the standing outside editor, and published to paying members an hour ahead of the open list. It is a small machine, but it runs.

Topics are not selected by popularity. They are chosen because a decision interests us — a fork in the road, a refusal, a tradition being rejected or extended. Some weeks that is a famous product; other weeks it is an obscure one. Readers have asked for a publishing calendar; we have declined to produce one.

Ethics & Ethos

Monograph accepts a single weekly underwriter. Underwriting lines appear at the top of the Tuesday email. They are flagged as such, they contain no tracking pixels, and they never appear inside the essay itself. We have turned down sponsors whose products we would not be willing to discuss critically on another week; we plan to keep doing so.

We do not take equity, advisory positions, or paid consulting from products we have written about in the past eighteen months, and we disclose prior relationships on the page when relevant. Corrections are posted, not silently edited, at the foot of each affected issue.

Your email address is stored in a single subscriber database we control. We do not share it, we do not segment it, we do not enrich it, and we purge unsubscribes within thirty days. Monograph uses no third-party tracking on the web archive — not analytics, not advertising, not heatmaps, not session replay.

The Colophon

Monograph is set in Instrument Serif for display, Newsreader for body copy, and JetBrains Mono for metadata. The web edition is hand-built in plain HTML and CSS; there is no framework, no build step, and almost no JavaScript. Hosting is static, with a strict content security policy. The annual book is printed on Mohawk Superfine Eggshell 80 lb text, Smyth-sewn, with a cloth-over-board cover.

The mark is a lowercase m with an oversized terminal, drawn by hand and occasionally revised. We reserve the right to revise it again.

Contact

Pitches, corrections, compliments, and withering attacks are all welcome at editor@monograph.press. Sponsorship inquiries at underwriting@monograph.press. We answer personally, which is to say slowly and in full sentences.
